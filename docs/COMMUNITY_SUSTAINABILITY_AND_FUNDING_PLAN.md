# Cartogen AI Community Edition — Private Sustainability and Funding Plan

**Classification:** Private HTTP-Solution commercial strategy
**Public-repository status:** Do not publish or synchronize this document to `cartogen_ai_community`.
**Purpose:** Define how the free GPL-2.0 Community edition can generate sustainable business value, funding, and ecosystem growth without undermining its usefulness or misrepresenting open-source rights.

## 1. Executive position

The Community edition should remain a useful, independently valuable GPL-2.0 QGIS plugin. It should not become a crippled trial, a disguised lead form, or a product that requires payment for basic local use.

Its commercial value comes from four outcomes:

1. **Adoption:** more GIS professionals use Cartogen AI.
2. **Trust:** public code, tests, documentation, and transparent security reduce buyer risk.
3. **Demand discovery:** usage, feedback, and sector workflows reveal what customers need.
4. **Conversion to paid value:** organizations pay for managed operations, support, governance, integrations, training, and deployment—not for the right to use GPL software.

The objective is not to extract maximum revenue from every Community user. The objective is to create a sustainable open-core ecosystem in which the Community edition expands reach and the commercial service captures operational value.

## 2. Community business gaps

### C1 — No explicit sustainability funnel

The Community edition has adoption and release discipline, but no defined path from download to:

- documentation support;
- sponsorship;
- paid training;
- implementation services;
- managed Cartogen AI access;
- organization deployment;
- grant-funded programme work.

### C2 — No separation between user value and sponsor value

Individual users need a reliable free tool. Institutions, funders, and sponsors may pay for maintenance, roadmap influence, training, support, or measurable social impact.

These are different offers and should not be conflated.

### C3 — No funding portfolio

Relying on hosted-service conversion alone creates concentration risk. Cartogen AI should pursue a portfolio of:

- grants;
- institutional sponsorship;
- paid implementation;
- training;
- support contracts;
- hosted service;
- research partnerships;
- donor-funded public-good work.

### C4 — No impact evidence

Funders and institutional sponsors need evidence of public benefit:

- users and organizations reached;
- humanitarian workflows supported;
- time saved;
- maps/reports produced;
- countries or response contexts served;
- accessibility and inclusion outcomes;
- community contributions;
- public releases maintained.

### C5 — GPL/commercial boundary needs a funding narrative

The project needs plain private guidance for explaining that funding supports maintenance, operations, stewardship, and services while GPL rights remain respected.

## 3. Community sustainability model

### Layer 1 — Free Community adoption

Keep free:

- GPL-2.0 plugin code;
- local Ollama operation;
- documented Cartogen AI hosted connection;
- core QGIS workflows;
- public tests and security notes;
- public issue tracker and release information;
- contribution and extension pathways.

Do not require payment for local use, basic installation, or the right to run modified GPL software.

### Layer 2 — Voluntary support

Offer optional support mechanisms:

- individual donations;
- recurring sponsorship;
- institutional sponsorship;
- GitHub Sponsors or equivalent platform;
- named release sponsorship;
- humanitarian emergency maintenance sponsorship;
- equipment or infrastructure sponsorship.

Supporters receive recognition, progress reports, and community benefits—not proprietary control over GPL code.

### Layer 3 — Paid expertise

Monetize professional time and operational responsibility:

- training workshops;
- QGIS/Cartogen onboarding;
- workflow design;
- humanitarian mapping implementation;
- data preparation and migration;
- report/dashboard automation;
- custom integrations;
- deployment and hardening;
- priority support;
- service reviews.

The fee is for expertise, delivery, support, and accountability—not a licence fee for GPL code.

### Layer 4 — Managed Cartogen AI service

Offer managed value through:

- hosted model access;
- provider-key management;
- budgets and rate limits;
- organization governance;
- audit visibility;
- identity integration;
- usage reporting;
- support and reliability;
- controlled/private deployment.

Community users may choose local Ollama and remain independent of the commercial service.

### Layer 5 — Funded public-good work

Seek funding for work that benefits the Community ecosystem:

- humanitarian mapping workflows;
- accessibility improvements;
- offline/low-connectivity operation;
- data-protection improvements;
- documentation and training;
- language localization;
- reproducible geospatial analysis;
- disaster-response templates;
- public-sector capacity-building;
- security and maintenance releases.

Where grant terms require open outputs, document the licence and release obligations before accepting funding.

## 4. Profit and funding channels

### 4.1 Institutional sponsorship

Target:

- NGOs;
- humanitarian consortia;
- foundations;
- GIS professional associations;
- universities;
- public-interest technology programmes;
- corporate geospatial/technology sponsors.

Sponsorship packages may fund:

- one year of maintenance;
- a named sector workflow pack;
- security hardening;
- accessibility/localization;
- documentation and training;
- release engineering;
- emergency response support capacity.

Deliverables should be public-impact reports, not private ownership of GPL code.

### 4.2 Grants and public funding

Potential grant themes:

- humanitarian innovation;
- crisis response and resilience;
- climate adaptation;
- open-source digital public goods;
- geospatial capacity development;
- digital inclusion;
- local-government modernization;
- open science and reproducibility;
- public health mapping;
- disaster risk reduction;
- open data and interoperability.

Grant applications should request funding for named outcomes:

- releases maintained;
- tested workflows delivered;
- organizations trained;
- documentation translated;
- security controls implemented;
- public datasets or synthetic demonstrations produced;
- measurable user impact achieved.

### 4.3 Paid training

Create a training catalogue:

- Cartogen AI Community fundamentals;
- safe spatial-AI use in QGIS;
- humanitarian 3W/4W mapping;
- coverage-gap analysis;
- engineering QA workflows;
- urban-planning accessibility analysis;
- logistics network mapping;
- prompt design for GIS analysts;
- reproducible map/report production;
- data protection and do-no-harm in mapping.

Possible buyers:

- NGOs;
- universities;
- government departments;
- consultancies;
- humanitarian training programmes;
- donor-funded capacity-building projects.

### 4.4 Paid implementation and consulting

Offer fixed-scope services:

- setup and onboarding;
- workflow audit;
- data model preparation;
- QGIS project templates;
- map/report automation;
- field data integration;
- M365/SharePoint/Power BI integration;
- deployment hardening;
- operational handover.

Use standardized scopes and change control to avoid unprofitable bespoke work.

### 4.5 Support subscriptions

Offer optional support plans for organizations using the Community edition:

- response-time commitments;
- installation assistance;
- upgrade guidance;
- troubleshooting;
- workflow review;
- training credits;
- security advisory notifications.

Do not imply that paid support changes GPL rights or grants exclusive ownership.

### 4.6 Hosted-service conversion

Community adoption can convert to managed Cartogen AI when the organization needs:

- no provider-key management;
- centralized budgets;
- shared access;
- organization identity;
- usage oversight;
- support;
- private deployment;
- contractual accountability.

Conversion messaging should be positive and operational, not fear-based or feature-crippling.

### 4.7 Research and university partnerships

Possible models:

- sponsored research workflows;
- student and faculty training;
- co-developed evaluation studies;
- geospatial-AI reproducibility projects;
- funded internships;
- applied humanitarian research;
- open benchmark datasets and workflow validation.

Use written agreements for data, publication, attribution, ethics, and software licensing.

### 4.8 Partner and reseller channel

Potential partners:

- QGIS consultants;
- humanitarian GIS firms;
- engineering consultancies;
- urban-planning practices;
- logistics optimization firms;
- training providers;
- cloud/VPS providers;
- local implementation partners.

Revenue can come from:

- referral fees;
- implementation margin;
- co-delivery;
- training delivery;
- managed-service resale;

### 4.9 Emergency and rapid-response retainers

Humanitarian organizations may pay for readiness rather than software alone:

- rapid mapping activation;
- emergency workflow support;
- surge analyst support;
- priority troubleshooting;
- situation-map production;
- crisis data integration.

Define availability, scope, response time, and surge capacity honestly.

## 5. Recommended revenue mix

Initial target mix for the first commercial year should be diversified rather than dependent on a large SaaS base:

- 30–40% managed Cartogen AI subscriptions;
- 20–30% implementation and workflow services;
- 10–20% training and support;
- 10–20% grants and institutional sponsorship;
- 5–15% research/partnership work.

These are planning targets, not forecasts. Validate them against actual sales, delivery effort, provider costs, and grant success.

## 6. Community-to-commercial funnel

### Stage 1 — Discover

User downloads the plugin, reads documentation, or sees a sector workflow.

Measure:

- downloads;
- installation success;
- provider connection attempts;
- documentation visits;
- sector interest.

### Stage 2 — Activate

User completes a first meaningful GIS workflow.

Measure:

- time to first successful task;
- task type;
- error rate;
- support request;
- local versus hosted provider choice.

### Stage 3 — Repeat

User returns for recurring mapping work.

Measure:

- weekly/monthly active usage where privacy allows;
- repeated workflow categories;
- saved workflow adoption;
- training requests;
- team use.

### Stage 4 — Support or sponsor

Organization requests help, training, implementation, or funds maintenance.

Measure:

- support conversion;
- training conversion;
- sponsorship conversations;
- grant leads;
- implementation scope.

### Stage 5 — Managed service

Organization requires governance, hosted access, integrations, or operational commitments.

Measure:

- paid activation;
- gross margin;
- support cost;
- retention;
- expansion;
- referral rate.

## 9. Guardrails

- Do not remove useful Community functionality merely to manufacture upgrade pressure.
- Do not charge for GPL rights that users already have.
- Do not promise exclusivity over GPL code without qualified legal review.
- Do not accept grant restrictions that conflict with the intended licence without review.
- Do not expose funder, donor, beneficiary, or humanitarian-sensitive data.
- Do not use Community telemetry without documented consent, minimization, and privacy review.
- Do not present sponsorship as ownership of the project.
- Do not promise public impact that cannot be measured.
- Do not let funded custom work silently create an unsupported permanent maintenance burden.
- Keep public messaging positive and transparent; keep private commercial mechanics in the private repository.

## 10. Success criteria

The Community edition sustainability model is working when:

- the free edition remains independently useful;
- maintenance has at least two funding sources;
- HTTP-Solution has repeatable training and implementation offers;
- at least one sponsor or grant funds public-good work;
- at least three organizations have paid for expertise or managed operational value;
- Community adoption and impact can be measured without invasive surveillance;
- public/private boundaries remain clear;
- Community improvements continue even when a particular commercial deal ends.
